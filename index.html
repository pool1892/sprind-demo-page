Two new jobs from a robot's existing skills

A simulated robot arm whose policy never changes was given two jobs it has no single skill for: leave the bowl on the cabinet with the drawer open, and leave the plate at the stove with the bowl on the cabinet. An AI engineering agent found why the plain chain of the robot's skills failed, tested one change at a time and built each job. On 20 fresh arrangements of the scene, held back until the end, both jobs delivered on all 20; the plain chains delivered on 1 and 5. The jobs, their checks and the agent's learned skills stay in the installation's library.

Transcript

0:00 New jobs, made reliable. The robot's policy never changes. An engineering agent improves the installation around it.

0:07 Job 1: the request. First job: the bowl on the cabinet and the middle drawer open, together, on at least 18 of 20 fresh starts. The robot has a skill for each half.

0:18 Job 1: what fails. Chained plainly, the hand turns to the drawer still touching the bowl, and knocks it off. On the agent's practice starts: 5 of 10.

0:28 Job 1: the experiment. The agent writes a check: does the bowl stay in place while the next skill runs? Then it changes one thing at a time. Open, then go home: 5 of 10. Let the placement run 30 steps longer, then go home: 10 of 10.

0:45 Job 1: released. Confirmed 20 of 20 on unused starts, then 20 of 20 reserved starts, against 1 for the plain chain: 19 rescued, 0 broken.

1:00 Job 1: what the installation keeps. It keeps the job, the check, and a lesson: an open, still hand at one instant has not cleared the path that follows.

1:10 Job 2: the request. Second job: the plate at the front of the stove and the bowl on the cabinet. Again, at least 18 of 20.

1:19 Job 2: what fails. Plate first, then bowl: 7 of 10 practice starts. Here the plate push runs out of time short of the stove.

1:27 Job 2: the experiment. The agent extends the first job's check to both goals and tests changes. Going home between skills: 8 of 10 delivered. Loading the cheese into the bowl gets every plate to the stove, but the loaded bowl becomes the bottleneck: 4 of 10 delivered.

1:47 Job 2: the scene, prepared. So the agent grasps the cheese, slides it aside onto the table, and leaves the bowl empty: 10 of 10 delivered.

1:59 Job 2: released. Confirmed 20 of 20 on unused starts, then 20 of 20 reserved starts, against 5 for the plain chain: 15 rescued, 0 broken. Here the plain chain reaches the stove, but its bowl attempt leaves neither goal standing.

2:16 Job 2: what the installation keeps. It keeps the job, the extended check, and a lesson: preparing the scene can move the bottleneck to the next skill.

2:24 The instrument. Story two: a fleet of arms at several sites, with the same lag at one site and on one robot elsewhere. The agent builds its own probe, commanded against measured: one lag is a fixed network delay, the other a controller falling behind.

2:41 The fleet, repaired. After paired experiments it compensates for the delay, rolls back an update and services the controller. On 576 reserved attempts it never saw, success rises from 451 to 558: 113 rescued, 6 broken.

3:01 The record. Two jobs at 20 and 20 of 20 reserved starts, and a fleet improved by an instrument the agent built. The policy never changed; every clip is a recorded run.
